## 2. Proposed slide: "How Yuno differs from a standalone vault"

Kicker: 04 · Versus a standalone vault · the difference. This extends Samuel's slide 16 without changing his quotes.

Headline: A standalone vault is built to store and forward. The rules and connections above it, which decide where each request goes, are yours to write and maintain.

Left column, "In their own words" (all PUBLIC DOCS, read 8 Oct 2026):

- VGS: "VGS does not make routing decisions for you." and "VGS powers orchestration, but does not act as the orchestrator." (verygoodsecurity.com/payment-orchestration, FAQ section)
- VGS: "Your application keeps ownership of provider selection, routing, and failover logic." (docs.verygoodsecurity.com/integrating-a-second-payment-provider)
- Basis Theory: its vault "operates independently of any payment processor or orchestration layer." (Series B announcement, 14 October 2025, as republished by the Merchant Risk Council)
- Basis Theory: the Payments Routing guide reads "This guide is currently under construction." (developers.basistheory.com/docs/card-payments/payments-routing)
- Basis Theory: "Basis Theory will facilitate creating your TRID (Token Requestor ID) with each of the Networks on your behalf." (developers.basistheory.com/docs/features/network-tokens). A real strength; we say so.

Middle column, "The one difference that matters": With a standalone vault, the token is the product; everything that decides where the token goes is yours. With Yuno, the same PCI DSS Level 1 vault sits under a rules layer that already exists for card payments: conditions on country, amount, BIN, brand and your metadata, percentage splits, fallback steps on decline, error or timeout, and Monitors that move traffic away and bring it back (CONFIRMED, docs.y.uno Routing and Monitors). For banking, you set the provider on every call today (CONFIRMED); rules for banks and issuers are open item 2 (OPEN, Product).

Right column, "What stays on your roadmap with a standalone vault" (each line is something the two vendors say or document the merchant builds or brings):

- The rules for which bank, issuer or processor each request goes to, and their upkeep.
- Retries, fallbacks and failover between providers.
- Every provider integration, written against each provider's own API through the proxy, and its maintenance when the API changes.
- Moving money: accounts, transfers and payouts.
- Recovery after a decline: subscription retries, authentication, and the network transaction ID chain across providers.

So what (one sentence): You already asked the hard question, who decides. With a standalone vault the answer is you, in code you maintain; with Yuno it is you, in rules you configure for cards today, per call for banks today, and by rule for banks as an open item.

Speaker notes (customer-readable): "Both vendors are good at what they say they do, and both are Yuno partners. The quotes are theirs, read on 8 October 2026, and we would ask you to confirm each one with them. The difference we want you to test is not the vault; it is what happens the day after the vault is live, when a second bank, a third issuer or a processor change needs a decision. Ask each of us who writes that rule, who maintains the integration it runs on, and what it costs you in engineering time."

## 4. Two strengths of each competitor to concede on the slide, in their words

Basis Theory

1. Token requestor ownership and portability: "Basis Theory will facilitate creating your TRID (Token Requestor ID) with each of the Networks on your behalf. Once created, you'll be able to use this ID with any provider in the future." (developers.basistheory.com/docs/features/network-tokens, read 8 Oct 2026). Our requestor model is open; theirs is stated.
2. A programmable vault: Reactors run the merchant's own code inside Basis Theory's environment, and the Proxy has request and response transforms; "any HTTP request can be sent through the Proxy" (developers.basistheory.com/docs/concepts/what-is-the-proxy, read 8 Oct 2026). Yuno has no equivalent in its docs.

Optional third, if space allows: data residency, "Data created in a region never leaves that region" (developers.basistheory.com/docs/features/global-data).

VGS

1. Network reach: "VGS is the ONLY non-PSP, neutral vault connected to all 4 major networks for network tokens." (verygoodsecurity.com/network-tokens, read 8 Oct 2026). Yuno documents three networks and cryptograms for two.
2. Scale and breadth of the vault itself: "10 billion tokens stored" (press release, 22 Sep 2026); inbound and outbound HTTPS routes, SFTP and ISO 8583 proxies, VGS Compute for custom code, and thirteen alias formats including format-preserving ones (docs.verygoodsecurity.com, read 8 Oct 2026). Yuno's proxy is outbound only and in beta.

House rules applied: both vendors are Yuno partners; the concessions are stated as facts with URLs, not softened and not sharpened. Competitor pricing stays in speaker notes.
