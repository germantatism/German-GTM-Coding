## 0. German's question, answered plainly

Question: if Carry1st integrates EBANX directly, does ONE integration and ONE contract switch on all five markets (Brazil, Argentina, Mexico, Colombia, Bolivia) at once, or does each country need separate enablement, documents or time?

Answer (PUBLIC DOCS, EBANX documentation):
* One integration: yes, technically. The Direct API uses the same request paths for every country; the country is a field in the payment object (sample request shows "country": "ar"; the docs say "Select another country from the dropdown if needed"). The cross-border pay-in model covers 23 countries and includes all five. The local model (funds settled to a local entity, "-local-latam hosts") exists only for Brazil, Chile, Colombia and Mexico, so Argentina and Bolivia are cross-border only. Source: docs.ebanx.com api-operability and multi-country credit-cards pages.
* One contract: EBANX publishes a single merchant agreement for international processing (pay-in and payout) and sets fees in the contract ("services agreed upon during the contracting process will follow the values stipulated in the contract", pricing page excerpt, 7 Aug 2024). Settlement currencies are "defined per merchant agreement". No per-country contract is documented. The terms excerpt (search index, page did not render) adds two operational facts: merchants must complete onboarding within 30 days of the agreement start, and services stay unavailable until onboarding concludes even if an integration key was issued. OPEN, owner German: confirm both clauses with Cordel's EBANX contact or the executed agreement.
* But enablement is not self-serve, and each country adds work:
  * "During onboarding, EBANX will configure the supported countries, payment methods, and settlement parameters for your account" (DEUNA partner page). Countries are switched on by EBANX, per account.
  * Go-live checklist: "You have successfully processed test transactions in the sandbox for all countries you intend to operate in" (DEUNA page). The FAQ says enabling payments in additional countries means sandbox testing before going live (FAQ excerpt via search index; page did not render).
  * Production access comes after an Integration Review requested by email (sales.engineering@ebanx.com) and approval; no published SLA or day count (pay-in guide).
  * "Merchants must define their operating model in advance when planning an integration" (api-operability).
* What still differs per country even with one integration (PUBLIC DOCS, EBANX country overview pages):
  * Methods: Brazil cards, Pix, Boleto, Mercado Pago Wallet, PayPal, PicPay, NuPay, Pix Automático, Pix on File, Pix 4x Pagaleve · Mexico cards (incl. Carnet), OXXO Pay, SPEI, Mercado Pago Wallet, Aplazo · Colombia cards (incl. Codensa), PSE Avanza, Nequi, Bre-B, Efecty, Botón Bancolombia, Nequi Recurring · Argentina cards (incl. Naranja, Cabal), Rapipago, Pago Fácil, Mercado Pago Wallet, DEBIN; currencies ARS and USD · Bolivia: PagosNet cash and PagosNet bank only; no cards listed (status page component for Bolivia is "Cash" only).
  * Customer documents: Brazil CPF required · Colombia document required (CC default, NIT, CE) · Argentina document required (DNI, CUIT for DEBIN, CUIL) · Mexico optional (CURP, RFC) · Bolivia refunds need a carnet de identidad and local bank data.
  * Colombia local model requires IVA data in every request; Argentina card refunds "may range from 89 to 120 days"; settlement and FX terms per agreement.
* What this means for slide 03: say it straight. EBANX direct can switch on the five markets with one integration and one agreement, provided EBANX enables each country on the account and Carry1st passes sandbox tests per country. Then win on the rows EBANX cannot change: no cards in Bolivia, no MODO or interoperable QR in Argentina, one provider with no routing, retries or failover, one approval rate, one negotiating position, tokens and checkout tied to one vendor, and a second provider later means a second integration project.

## 3. Yuno, the facts (CONFIRMED unless stated)

### Integration path
* Four integration types: seamless-sdk (Full Checkout, recommended, add methods without code, configure checkout in the dashboard), lite-sdk (merchant controls method display), headless-sdk (merchant builds all UI), secure-fields (web). Server-to-server payment creation with public and private keys. Source: docs.y.uno/docs/sdks/overview/choose-integration; create-payment guides.
* Connections are credentials entered in the dashboard in a three-step flow (credentials and optional 3DS, optional costs for Smart Routing and reconciliation, accounts), or created by API (provider catalog + create connection, with a pre-flight credential check). EBANX and dLocal are both in the provider catalog (api-enums). Source: docs connections page; reference/organizations/connections.
* Routing: condition sets by country, currency, amount, BIN, brand, installments, transaction type (CIT, MIT, RECURRING) or metadata; each route is a list of provider steps; a step's output on DECLINED, a decline group, TIMEOUT, INTERNAL_ERROR or ERROR_RATE jumps to the next step (this is the one-time-payment retry on a second provider); percentage splits across providers; Smart Routing (AI) for the rest. Routes are editable after publishing. Source: docs routing; reference routing-resource-shape.
* Monitors: when a provider's approval rate drops below the threshold, traffic redirects to the fallback provider, then a second fallback, then whichever provider has the highest approval rate; a small share stays on the original to detect recovery; alerts by email or Opsgenie. Source: docs monitors; automation.
* Retries: route-step cascade for one-time payments (above); Smart Retries (up to six attempts) for Yuno-managed subscriptions; Transaction Retries (up to seven attempts) for failed captures and refunds. Source: docs automation.
* Vault and tokens: vaulted tokens have "cross-processor portability: a single vaulted token works across all processors connected to your Yuno account"; network tokens provisioned automatically for enrolled cards (Visa, Mastercard) and auto-updated; the network transaction id is stored on the token so a stored card is portable across providers; token and network-token migration processes exist. Source: docs pci-compliance; tokens; network-tokens; stored-credentials.
* Dashboard and insights: approval, retry, chargeback analytics by provider, brand, country; reconciliation reports (Reconciliation Overview, Settlements and Fees, Settlement Payouts); Payment Concierge (AI analyst in Slack, WhatsApp, Teams, Telegram) and Maia (AI technical account manager). Source: docs insights; reconciliations; ai-capabilities.
* Caveat to carry into the launch plan: a route with a fallback is not supported when the primary provider uses "Provider installments" (different installment plans per provider); card installments in Brazil need a Yuno-managed plan or no fallback on that step. Source: docs provider-installments.
* Implementation time: not published in docs. y.uno homepage: "90% reduction in development time" and "Reduction in development time: 90%". Rappi success story (21 Nov 2024): "Yuno cut Rappi's implementation time for new providers down to zero". Internal planning figures in CONTEXT.md (connections 1 to 2 days once credentials exist, integration and QA 3 to 5 days) are ESTIMATE, Yuno.

## 5. The three beats, adapted from the Replit slides and the Eventbrite "proposed model"

Source files reused: yuno-sales-pitch-maker/src/components/slides/SlideReplitBenefits.jsx and SlideReplitGoingGlobal.jsx (three beats and the EBANX vs dLocal mini-cards); Deals/Eventbrite/claude-design-prompt-proposed-model.md (TODAY vs OPTION A vs OPTION B grid with red X, orange tilde, green check and a dark takeaway bar).

1. One integration unlocks every MoR. EBANX covers Brazil, Mexico, Colombia and Argentina with cards and the big local rails, but Bolivia is cash-only and Argentina lacks MODO and QR. dLocal fills exactly those gaps. Through Yuno both are connections on one integration, with 33 to 57 more providers per country waiting for Phase 2. Punchline: "EBANX for what it covers, dLocal for what it does not, one integration."
2. One dashboard, plug and play. Approval rates, retries, settlements, chargebacks and reconciliation across EBANX and dLocal in one console, with Payment Concierge answering "why did Brazil approval drop this week" in Slack. No second portal when the second provider arrives.
3. MoR swap insurance. If EBANX pricing or service slips, Carry1st routes Brazil to dLocal with a rule, not a sprint. Tokens stay in Yuno's vault, network tokens included. Caveat to state in notes: cash and voucher methods have nothing to tokenize, and a provider-installments route cannot carry a fallback.
