# SDR Research Brief — Babbel (babbel.com / Lesson Nine GmbH)
*Yuno Payment Orchestrator · Framework v8.0 · 2026-07-27 · accuracy-first*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Babbel is a Berlin-based language-learning subscription business (€352M revenue FY2024, +6.6% YoY; 18M subscriptions sold in 2024; ~25M cumulative) selling globally, with the US as its single largest market (36% of traffic). The load-bearing finding: Babbel runs a **multi-PSP recurring-billing stack (Adyen confirmed first-party + a reported Adyen→Stripe migration of $100M+ recurring revenue) with no payment orchestrator**, and it executes failed-payment recovery PSP-side (Adyen Auto Rescue) rather than across all processors. Its APM coverage is thin and geo-gated (only iDEAL in NL and Klarna in BE; PayPal explicitly unavailable in Turkey and Brazil; no German-local methods listed in its home market). The primary Yuno opportunity is an orchestration layer over Adyen + Stripe for auth-rate uplift, cross-processor decline/involuntary-churn recovery, and local-APM expansion, landing against a new-CEO cash-efficiency + AI mandate. **ICP score: 16 → 🔴 High.**

## SECTION 10 — Strategic Insights

**Insight #1: Multi-PSP, zero orchestration**
Evidence: S3 — Adyen (confirmed) + Stripe (reported migration) + Shopify (gifts), no orchestrator; a manual consultant-led $100M+ migration. Pain Point: PSP lock-in, costly hard-coded migrations, no single control plane. Yuno Value Prop: one API over both processors, no-code routing, switch/add PSPs without re-integration. Best Case: Reserva (+4% approval <3 months). Outreach Angle: You already run more than one processor and paid to migrate between them; an orchestration layer makes the next processor change a config, not a project.

**Insight #2: Involuntary churn is a first-party, documented problem**
Evidence: S5 — auto-renewal surprise charges, decline-then-lapse, account-updater re-billing; Adyen Auto Rescue only recovers within Adyen. Pain Point: lost recurring revenue from failed renewals and expired cards. Yuno Value Prop: cross-processor smart retries + account updater + network tokens; up to 50% transaction recovery, +7% approval. Best Case: Livelo (+5% approval, 50% recovery). Outreach Angle: Recovery that spans every processor, not just one PSP's dunning.

**Insight #3: Thin, geo-gated local methods across large markets**
Evidence: S4 — only iDEAL (NL) and Klarna (BE); no local method in Germany (home), France, Italy, Brazil; PayPal blocked in BR/TR. Pain Point: conversion left on the table where cards are not first choice. Yuno Value Prop: 1,000+ methods via one integration, live per market in weeks. Best Case: InDrive (10 LATAM markets <8 months). Outreach Angle: Expand the local-method set market by market from one API.

**Insight #4: Cash-efficiency + AI mandate under a new CEO**
Evidence: S6 — Tim Allen, IPO off the table, cost discipline, no VP Payments (payments under CFO Baier). Pain Point: margin and payment cost are board-level. Yuno Value Prop: lower processing cost via routing to the cheapest performant processor + reduced eng spend on integrations. Outreach Angle: CFO-persona framing on margin and avoided engineering cost.

**Insight #5: Cross-border in top EU markets**
Evidence: S2 — no local entity in France/Italy/UK. Pain Point: cross-border MDR + no local acquiring. Yuno Value Prop: local acquiring/MoR without standing up entities. Outreach Angle: local economics without local incorporation.
