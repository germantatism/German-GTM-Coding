## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Intuit (TurboTax, QuickBooks, Credit Karma, Mailchimp) closed FY26 (July 2026) at **$21.45B revenue, +14%**, with international at only about 8% of revenue [S42][S43]. Intuit is itself a payments provider. QuickBooks Payments runs on Intuit Payments Inc. (a licensed money transmitter), and its US legal terms include a Chase Paymentech agreement [S5]. Online payment volume passed **$225B in FY26**, and Intuit is hiring engineers to migrate merchants *off* Stripe, Braintree, PayPal, Square and Adyen [S8][S44]. Outside the US, though, the merchant stack differs in every market: **Adyen NV in the UK**, **PayPal, GoCardless and Square integrations in Australia**, and Intuit Payments in Canada [S36][S37][S38]. No orchestrator was found.

The realistic Yuno opportunity is **not** the US QuickBooks Payments core, which Intuit builds in-house. It sits in two places:
- **Mailchimp's global self-serve subscription billing.** It becomes a separate reportable segment in FY27, with growth flat to down 1%. Declines come back with no reason, and repeated declines lock the account for 24 hours [S19][S20][S43].
- **The fragmented international QuickBooks Payments footprint**, which is a platform and partner angle.

## SECTION 10: Strategic Insights

**Insight #1: Intuit is a PSP, so lead with the edges, not the core**
- **Evidence:** In Section 3A, US money movement runs on Intuit Payments Inc. and a Chase Paymentech agreement [S5]. Intuit is hiring to pull merchants off Stripe, Braintree, PayPal, Square and Adyen [S8], and it is adding its own rails (FedNow, Affirm, USDC) [S21][S22][S23].
- **Pain Point:** None for Yuno in the US merchant core. Intuit sees itself as the processor there.
- **Yuno Value Prop:** Do not pitch replacing QuickBooks Payments. Pitch orchestration where Intuit is the *merchant* (Mailchimp and subscription billing) and where it relies on third parties (international).
- **Best Case:** Rappi ("implementation time for new providers down to zero").
- **Outreach Angle:** "You've built world-class rails in the US. We help on the edges you don't want to build: the long tail of international acquirers and payment methods."

**Insight #2: International QuickBooks Payments is a different stack in every market**
- **Evidence:** In Section 3A, the UK runs on Adyen NV [S37][S24]; Australia relies on PayPal, GoCardless and Square integrations [S38]; Canada runs on Intuit Payments [S36]. International is about 8% of revenue [S42].
- **Pain Point:** There are separate integrations, pricing and reporting per market, and launching a new market means another bespoke partner. That partly explains the exits from India, Brazil, Mexico and France [INFERENCE, not confirmed].
- **Yuno Value Prop:** One integration for local acquiring and payment methods in 190+ countries. New markets go live in weeks with no-code PSP enablement.
- **Best Case:** inDrive ("around 90% approval, new countries in under eight months").
- **Outreach Angle:** "Every new QuickBooks Payments market today means a new partner integration; we turn that into a configuration."

**Insight #3: Mailchimp's global billing is the clearest orchestration buyer**
- **Evidence:** Section 6 shows Mailchimp as a standalone reportable segment with flat FY27 guidance [S43]. Section 5 shows declines with no reason and a 24-hour lock after more than four declines [S19][S20]. Section 4A shows region-locked methods and Amex limited to 5 currencies [S32][S20]. Mailchimp is Intuit's "tip of the spear" for international growth [S25].
- **Pain Point:** Involuntary churn on a self-serve international base, at the moment the segment has to show its own P&L.
- **Yuno Value Prop:** Smart retries and routing (up to 7% authorization uplift, 30% recovered revenue), local acquiring to cut cross-border declines, and local methods activated per market without engineering work.
- **Best Case:** Yuno subscriptions engine: 16 merchants, recovery and reactivation figures (see the internal subs stats reference before quoting).
- **Outreach Angle:** "With Mailchimp reporting on its own from FY27, every recovered renewal shows up directly in the segment."

**Insight #4: Subscription billing reliability (double charges and stale cards)**
- **Evidence:** Section 5 shows QBO double billing 2020 to May 2026 and old cards still charged after an update [S14][S16].
- **Pain Point:** Refund handling, support cost and brand damage on a product sold on trust.
- **Yuno Value Prop:** Real-time monitors and one view across providers. Rappi went from 5–10 min of manual detection to milliseconds, with 80% less analyst resolution time.
- **Best Case:** Rappi.
- **Outreach Angle:** Use only in discovery, not cold outreach (the complaint volume is modest).

**Insight #5: Stablecoins and new rails need routing logic**
- **Evidence:** Section 7 shows the Circle USDC partnership (Dec 2025) and FedNow certification [S22][S23].
- **Pain Point:** Each new rail (USDC, FedNow, Affirm) adds routing and reconciliation decisions [INFERENCE, not confirmed].
- **Yuno Value Prop:** Yuno's operating system orchestrates payment methods, processors and stablecoins in one layer.
- **Best Case:** N/A (no public stablecoin case verified).
- **Outreach Angle:** Use for a follow-up email, not the opener.
