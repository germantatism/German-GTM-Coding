## 1. Summary: what has happened

The relationship started on June 26 with an intro call between Justo and Andrew. Andrew pushed for NDA, demo and terms within ten days, and the NDA was fully signed by Aug 3. On **Aug 11** Yuno ran the discovery and demo call and sent **Proposal 1**: Yuno's full platform as a CellPoint-branded white label at $50,000/month, $0.07 per successful transaction and a 50/50 share of net platform revenue on every deal CellPoint closes. Andrew's reaction on the call was "this is beyond an expectation." No written feedback ever arrived.

On **Sep 1**, on the call that was meant to lock an in-person workshop, Andrew explained why: replacing CellPoint's own orchestration engine (Velocity, built over 12+ years out of their India dev center) with a third party would undermine the asset value their shareholder, Tosca Penta, is counting on. He paused the white label ("that's not killing that by any means") and asked for an interim "aggregator solution": APM connections only, under Velocity, without touching their core. Yuno sent **Proposal 2** the same night: connections, not the engine, $15,000/month plus usage. Patrick opened it within minutes.

Since then CellPoint has gone quiet in writing. Andrew's only email (Sep 8) said he and Patrick "have not had time to meet to discuss" and would revert. On WhatsApp, Justo pinged on Sep 15 and 16 with "an updated idea" and "one opp"; Andrew replied he was locked into the T2RL conference and would share "what optimisation framework looks like" to "combine discussion on supporting your orchestration discussions," which he said is "different to the APM proposal."

Then the company changed shape. On **Sep 21** CellPoint announced **$34M from Toscafund** (via Penta Capital), a new leadership team (Kevin Murphy as Group CEO, Michael Kelly as Chairman, Patrick Uckermark as Chief Product and Technology Officer) and **Zenith**, an "AI decisioning platform" that "sits above a payment orchestrator, either CellPoint's own or one supplied by a third party." Fifty hires are planned in product, data, AI, sales and partnerships. Andrew is not named in the release; CellPoint's team page now lists him as Chief Partnerships and Marketing Officer.

On **Sep 24** Andrew reopened both proposals, and **Michael Kelly, the new Chairman, read both of them six times across one day**. On Sep 29, after two nudges from German, a "Kevin" answered in the WhatsApp group (Patrick on leave, "probably next week better"), and Andrew sent the Oct 7 invite himself. ⚠️ "Kevin" is almost certainly Kevin Murphy, the Group CEO; the chat shows him as a non-contact, so treat the identification as an inference until the call.

**Where it stands in one line:** two proposals on the table, zero written feedback in 57 days, the proposal now sitting with the Chairman and CEO of a company that has just told the market it sits above orchestrators rather than being one.

### Five things to know cold

1. **Proposal 2 is the live offer.** $15,000/month, $0.02 per transaction only on Yuno connections, four new APM integrations per quarter included, $500 per extra APM, no revenue share, 3-year term. Proposal 1 ($50,000/month white label) is paused, not dead.
2. **The reader has changed.** Michael Kelly (Chairman, appointed by Penta Capital) read both proposals on Sep 24. Kevin Murphy (Group CEO) is in the WhatsApp group. Patrick now owns product, technology and the AI and data roadmap. Andrew runs partnerships and marketing. The people who scoped the APM proposal are not the people who decide it.
3. **Zenith sits "above any orchestrator."** That is CellPoint's own positioning. It means the asset value Andrew protected on Sep 1 is now being built on the decisioning layer, not on Velocity. A Yuno connectivity layer underneath Zenith is complementary to the story they just sold to their investors.
4. **Andrew has signalled a second track.** His Sep 21 message separates "the APM proposal" from an "optimisation framework" that could support "your orchestration discussions." Read: CellPoint may want to offer Zenith-style decisioning to Yuno's airline merchants. Justo's "one opp" (Sep 16) is probably the first test case. ⚠️ Inference; confirm on the call.
5. **No written feedback in 57 days (Proposal 1) and 36 days (Proposal 2).** Every reply from CellPoint since Aug 11 has been about calendars. The one thing to extract on Wednesday is a verdict, or the name of the person who gives it.

**What changed between the two, and why.** Proposal 1 asked CellPoint to replace its engine. Andrew's objection on Sep 1 was not product or price; it was that "there is asset value on the balance sheet" and that swapping the engine for a third party "becomes quite a really complicated piece in terms of future value." Proposal 2 removes that problem by design: Velocity stays, Yuno sits underneath as the APM network. The per-transaction fee drops from $0.07 to $0.02 and only touches Yuno-routed traffic, the revenue share is gone, and the integration cadence is priced in (four per quarter, $500 beyond).

**What Proposal 2 does not yet have.** A scoped list of APMs. Andrew said on Sep 1 that an internal prospecting exercise would produce the number "on Friday." It never arrived. Without it the $0.02 and the four-per-quarter cadence cannot be sized, and neither side can model the deal.

**⚠️ Open discrepancy to clear before the call.** The Sep 1 design brief and the Sep 1 internal notes describe Proposal 2 as two side-by-side options (platform fee + 50/50 revenue share, or platform fee + $500 per integration, no per-transaction fee in either). The deck in Drive as of Sep 3 is a single structure with a $0.02 usage fee and no revenue share. The Oct 5 Pipeline Brief still describes the two-option version. Verify the Papermark PDF so the room is talking about the same numbers.

**How Zenith reframes both.** Zenith is described by CellPoint as a decisioning layer "above a payment orchestrator, either CellPoint's own or one supplied by a third party," deployable "without replacing their existing payment stack." That sentence makes Proposal 2 the natural fit (Yuno as connectivity under Velocity and Zenith) and gives Proposal 1 a new name if it ever comes back: Zenith on top of Yuno's orchestration, with CellPoint's asset value living in Zenith rather than in the engine. Do not pitch that reframing unprompted; let Andrew and Patrick describe Zenith first, then place Yuno in the architecture they draw.

## 5. What has happened at CellPoint these days

**The leadership triad is new, and it is Penta Capital's.** Steven Scott of Penta Capital put it on the record: "We backed the team and the plan." Kevin Murphy (Group CEO, a private-equity turnaround operator from lending, no travel background) and Michael Kelly (Chairman) were installed by the investor; Patrick Uckermark was elevated from CTO to Chief Product and Technology Officer with the AI and data roadmap. A Transformation Director (Mike Mayfield) and a new CFO (Ian Kelly) sit alongside them. Andrew Goddard, who drove this relationship from June to September, is now Chief Partnerships and Marketing Officer: still the door, no longer the product owner.

**The strategy moved from "we are the orchestrator" to "we sit above the orchestrator."** Zenith is sold as a decisioning layer that works above CellPoint's own engine or anyone else's, and the trade press noticed the company dropped "Digital" from its name in the same announcement. Internally that means the asset value Andrew protected on Sep 1 is now being counted on Zenith, and Velocity becomes plumbing. Externally it means CellPoint now wants to be deployed on top of other orchestrators, including, potentially, Yuno's merchants.

**The money changes the pressure, not the direction.** $34M and 50 hires buy time, but a new CEO who has just published a thesis has to show a proof point fast, and two analysts (quasa.io, The Industry Spread) have already written that Zenith has no named live deployment. APMs are the part of the stack Zenith does not build, and connectivity is the fastest proof CellPoint can put in front of an airline. That is Proposal 2's argument, and it is stronger today than on Sep 1.

**Andrew's two-track message.** His Sep 21 note draws the line himself: the APM proposal on one side, an "optimisation framework" to support "your orchestration discussions" on the other. Combined with Justo's "one opp where your approach can help," the most likely shape of Wednesday is a two-way conversation: Yuno supplies APM connectivity under Velocity and Zenith; CellPoint offers Zenith decisioning or payment optimisation consulting to Yuno's airline and travel merchants. ⚠️ Inference. Prepare for it; do not open with it.

**Reading of the silence.** Since Sep 8 every CellPoint message has been about timing, and the Papermark log shows the proposals being read at Chairman level on Sep 24, three days after the funding closed. The quiet is consistent with a board-level review of every vendor line in the first weeks of a new CEO, not with a lost deal. The invite came from Andrew unprompted on Sep 29, with the APM feedback named as agenda item one.

**What this means for Yuno on the call**

1. **Ask Patrick, not Andrew, for the verdict on Proposal 2.** Patrick owns product and the AI and data roadmap now, and he was the one who opened the proposal within seven minutes of receiving it.
2. **Let them draw Zenith first.** Then place Yuno where it fits: the connection network underneath, feeding Zenith the data it decides on. Yuno already exposes full transaction lifecycle data by API (the point Patrick cared most about on Aug 11).
3. **Hold the commercial structure.** Nothing CellPoint has said argues for a discount; the $34M argues the other way. If pricing is raised, Justo answers, and the only lever worth exploring is the APM scope (how many, which markets), which drives the four-per-quarter cadence and the $0.02 volume.
4. **Get the APM list.** It was promised on Sep 1 and never arrived. Without it there is no sizing, no timeline and no contract.
5. **Be ready for the reverse ask.** If Andrew or Kevin propose Zenith or an optimisation audit for Yuno merchants, the answer is interest plus a named test case, with Justo deciding which merchant. Yuno sells direct into airlines (Qatar Airways, Copa, Wingo; pipeline with Flair and FlightHub), so any joint motion needs a channel rule before a merchant name is shared.

**Landmines**

- Never say or imply Velocity is being replaced, commoditised, or that Zenith proves orchestration is a commodity. Their own press did that; let it stay theirs.
- Do not mention Papermark views, the Chairman's reading, or the Companies House filings.
- Do not congratulate Patrick on the title in front of Andrew; congratulate the company.
- Do not name a lower number or a new structure unless Justo says it.
- Jarrett worked four months inside CellPoint; insider commentary is his to make, not German's.
