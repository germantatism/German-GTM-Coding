# About Hostinger

## What they do

On the surface, a low-cost web hosting provider. In practice, a high-volume, low-ticket machine of prepaid subscriptions that over the last three years repositioned itself as a no-code and agentic AI platform for SMBs and solopreneurs.

Founded in 2004 in Kaunas, Lithuania, as Hosting Media; renamed Hostinger in 2011. Headquarters in Vilnius (the "Cyber City" campus), registered address in London. ICANN-accredited registrar, so it sells domains directly without intermediaries. Around 1,000 employees, roughly half in Lithuania.

**Leadership:** CEO Giedrius Zakaitis, appointed June 16, 2026 to lead the AI-first strategy (14-year insider, previously Chief Product and Technology Officer; predecessor Daugirdas Jankus moved to new strategic initiatives). Board chairman Arnas Stuopelis (was CEO until ~2023). CFO Domantas Beržanskis. COO Aivaras Šimkus. (Third-party databases like Growjo or GetLatka list outdated CEOs; the corporate blog is the reliable source.)

## Ownership

Bootstrapped, no venture capital. Majority of the equity sits with founders and long-tenure employees (which is why the €11.8M stock option payout in early 2026 makes sense). **ConHostinger GmbH** holds ~31% with board seats, bought in 2021; the real vehicle behind it is **Equivia Partners** (Cologne). It is a minority stake: Wikipedia's "controlling stake" wording is incorrect. **Tesonet** (the Lithuanian venture builder behind NordVPN, Oxylabs, Surfshark) is a strategic shareholder and growth accelerator, not a parent company.

**November 2025: dividend recapitalization.** They took on debt to pay dividends to shareholders. Read: the owners extract returns via leverage, there is no IPO on the horizon, and there is real pressure on cash flow and on cost per transaction.

## Business model

What they actually sell is **prepaid server time packaged into long terms, plus a growing software layer:**

- **Shared and WordPress hosting:** the volume of the business, from ~$1.99/mo promotional
- **Cloud:** Cloud Professional is the only sales-led motion in the company; everything else is pure self-serve
- **VPS/KVM and game servers:** from ~$5.84/mo, 12 and 24-month terms only, renewal jumps ~100%
- **Domains:** 300 extensions, first year at $0.01 to $1 on selected TLDs, .com renews at $14.99/year
- **Business email:** per-seat model, from ~$0.39/mailbox/mo renewing at ~$1.59
- **Website Builder / AI Builder:** from ~$2.99/mo, prompt-based site generation
- **Horizons:** AI app builder on consumption credits, 800K+ users in its first year; relaunched in August 2026 as the agentic mode of the AI Builder platform
- **Reach:** AI email marketing, 150K users as of December 2025
- **Agentic layer (2026):** managed OpenClaw and Hermes agents, self-hosted n8n, AI Router, Hosting Connector via MCP, CLI and SDK
- **Kodee:** the support AI that handled 81% of support interactions by end of 2025 (~€9M annual savings); being replaced by "Hostinger Agent" rolling out since mid-August 2026

**Pricing mechanics, the heart of the model:**

- Nearly all list prices require a **24 or 48-month commitment paid 100% upfront**
- Everything **auto-renews at 2x to 5x the intro price.** The clearest example: Business at $191.52 upfront for 48 months, renewing at $911.52 for the next 48
- A longer term does not lower the renewal rate, it only lowers the first term; advertised prices are pre-tax and checkout can add up to 20% VAT

**Distribution:** pure self-serve, no sales force. Three channels carry almost everything: SEO and content, affiliates (40% base commission, up to 60% on Horizons monthly plans, paid **only on the initial purchase**: renewals, domains, email and upgrades carry no commission), and YouTube creators.

**Unit economics:** the CAC is absorbed entirely in the first term (affiliate commission plus 70 to 80% discount off list), so **all the margin lives in the renewal.** The 48-month term is architecture, not generosity: the longer the commitment, the further out the cancel decision. A failed renewal charge is the single most expensive event in this model, which is why their RFP is roughly half about renewal authorization rates.

Three monetization models coexist: prepaid long-term subscription (hosting), per-seat (email), and usage-based credits (Horizons). The third is new and changes the nature of the business: variable revenue tied to AI usage, with real per-token COGS.

## Where they operate

Global self-serve footprint: **customers in 150+ countries, 13 data centers** across Asia, Europe and the Americas.

- **Top 5 markets by active users:** India, Brazil, United States, Indonesia, France
- **Also in the top 10:** Pakistan, United Kingdom, Spain, Mexico, Colombia

**Corporate structure:** parent entity Hostinger International Ltd, with brands 000webhost (free hosting, acquisition funnel), Hosting24 (cPanel brand for the US), Niagahoster (Indonesia, with its own local payments stack), Weblink (Brazil, local checkout with PIX, Boleto and installments) and Zyro (reabsorbed into the product as Website Builder / AI Builder).

Beyond the legal entities, they operate **dozens of country storefronts** (hostinger.com.br, .mx, .co, .com.ar, .in, .co.id, .ph, .pk, .de, .fr, .pl and more). They are not separate legal entities, but each one runs its own currency, its own payment method mix and likely its own acquiring relationship. The legal org chart has six boxes; the payments map has forty-plus checkouts. That asymmetry is, literally, the orchestration argument.

**Competition:** GoDaddy, IONOS, Namecheap, Bluehost, SiteGround, Kinsta, DigitalOcean at the technical end, Locaweb in Brazil; Wix, Squarespace and Shopify on builders and commerce. The promo-price-then-expensive-renewal dynamic is industry standard, not a Hostinger quirk (IONOS renews at 14x, SiteGround jumps 502%).

**Business risks:** dependence on SEO and affiliate traffic in an era where search is being reshaped by AI; revenue concentration in a model that depends on renewals not failing; debt from the 2025 recap; a strong bet that the agentic layer sustains pricing as pure infrastructure commoditizes; and a history of security incidents (000webhost 2015, ~14M customers affected in a 2019 breach).

## Why it matters for this deal

Their base is concentrated in markets with low international card penetration, fragmented local payment methods and high cross-border decline rates (India, Brazil, Indonesia, Pakistan, Mexico, Colombia). That is exactly the merchant profile where orchestration has the largest measurable impact. And since the margin engine is the auto-renewal, renewal authorization is the revenue line.
