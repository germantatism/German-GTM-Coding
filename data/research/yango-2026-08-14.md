## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Yango is a Dubai-headquartered ride-hailing, delivery, and multi-service super-app operating across 30+ countries, concentrated in Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, and Latin America, historically spun out of Yandex's ride-hailing business. The company is mid-expansion: it committed $150M to enter 10 additional African markets in 2026, completed the Otaxi acquisition in Oman (April 2025), and runs a $20M venture arm investing in adjacent African fintech/logistics startups. The clearest payments finding across all four research angles is a **complete absence of any public PSP, gateway, or orchestration-vendor signal** at the group level — no checkout source, job posting, or press release names a processor — while the news that *does* exist shows market-by-market, one-off payment partnerships (Flutterwave in Zambia, Peso/USDT in Bolivia, both local integrations, not a global rail). Combined with real, dated payment complaints (double charges, refund friction, difficulty cancelling subscription products), the picture is a company scaling fast into new markets with a fragmented, undisclosed, likely market-by-market payments stack rather than a unified one — exactly the shape of problem Yuno solves. No revenue, PSP, or CEO identity claim below should be used in outreach without the flagged verification.

## SECTION 10 — Strategic Insights

**Insight #1: The payments stack looks fragmented and market-by-market, not unified**
Evidence: Section 3A/3B — zero group-level PSP/orchestrator evidence across 20+ searches, but two hyper-local, market-specific deals found (Flutterwave in Zambia only, Peso in Bolivia only for one product line). | Pain Point: as Yango pushes into 10 more African markets in 2026 ($150M commitment, Section 6), each new market likely means another one-off local payments deal to strike and maintain, rather than one integration that extends automatically. | Yuno Value Prop: one API to bring local acquiring and APMs online per new market in weeks, no per-market vendor negotiation from scratch. | Best Case: inDrive (11 new countries launched in 8 months via Yuno) is the closest match — a peer-scale ride-hailing/emerging-market player with the same expansion cadence. | Outreach Angle: "As you commit $150M to enter 10 new African markets this year, is each one requiring a new local payments partnership from scratch, or is there a single rail extending with you?"

**Insight #2: Real, dated payment-friction complaints exist and cluster around double-charging and refund/subscription-cancellation friction**
Evidence: Section 5 — multiple dated PissedConsumer/Trustpilot/App Store reports (double charges, denied refunds, recurring charges on unused subscriptions, forced card→cash fallback with no retry). | Pain Point: reconciliation and retry logic gaps that show up directly in customer-facing complaints, a reputational as well as operational cost. | Yuno Value Prop: centralized transaction monitoring/reconciliation and retry orchestration reduce exactly this class of issue. | Best Case: Rappi (40-60% less manual reconciliation, engineering refocused on core product). | Outreach Angle: "We noticed a recurring pattern in your reviews around double charges and refund delays — that's usually a reconciliation/retry gap, not a support-team problem. Worth a conversation?"

**Insight #3: No confirmed orchestration or even single-PSP evidence at all — a genuinely unusual data gap that itself signals opportunity**
Evidence: Section 3B. | Pain Point: whether by design (deliberately opaque, in-app-only) or by fragmentation (many local one-off deals), there is no evidence of a company-wide payments strategy visible from outside. | Yuno Value Prop: consolidating visibility and control under one dashboard/data layer, regardless of how many local processors sit behind it. | Best Case: Livelo (single dashboard, standardized data, centralized KPIs replacing legacy portals and spreadsheets). | Outreach Angle: frame as a discovery question rather than an assumption — do not claim "you have no orchestrator," ask whether payments visibility keeps pace with the market-expansion pace.

**Insight #4: Rapid, capital-backed expansion into secondary/underserved markets is the active growth thesis right now**
Evidence: Section 6 — $150M/10-country Africa push, $20M venture arm, Otaxi acquisition, explicit "secondary cities, not head-on competition" strategy. | Pain Point: entering underserved/secondary markets often means weaker default card infrastructure and higher reliance on local APMs/mobile money from day one. | Yuno Value Prop: day-one local coverage in markets where global PSPs often lag. | Best Case: inDrive again (local acquiring edge in emerging markets). | Outreach Angle: tie directly to the $150M announcement — timely and specific, not generic.

**Insight #5: Ownership/entity structure is unusually opaque post-Yandex split — worth clarifying early in any deal process**
Evidence: Section 2 — conflicting entity names (Ridetech International B.V. historically, RideTechnology Global FZ-LLC currently), Nebius Group split with no clear current cap table found, unverified GDPR fine reference. | Pain Point: not a payments pain point directly, but relevant to deal/compliance diligence — worth understanding who actually signs a contract before going deep. | Yuno Value Prop: N/A — this is a process note, not a sales angle. | Outreach Angle: none — internal flag only, resolve via discovery call, not outreach copy.
